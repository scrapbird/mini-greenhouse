# Study guide — mini-greenhouse build

Skills you'll touch, in roughly the order you need them. Each entry: what, why, links.

---

## 1. Measure & mark

What: pull tape, transfer dim to timber, mark cut line with square + pencil. "Measure twice, cut once" is real.

Why: every cut later inherits the error from this step.

Watch:
- [Tape measure tips for beginners](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ifk7Bn5Vluc) — reading 16ths, hook is loose on purpose
- [Using a combination square](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2hkqe_VFOR0) — square line, mitre, marking depth

---

## 2. Crosscuts (square cuts to length)

What: 90° cut across timber. Most cuts on this build are crosscuts to length.

Tools: hand saw OR circular saw with speed square as guide.

Why: posts, rails, slats — all crosscut.

Watch:
- [Circular saw basics (beginner)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R4ApY7lFqEM)
- [Using a speed square as a cut guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K8GG8FXxnGw)
- [Hand saw technique](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4iJKBYNJxqM) — if going manual

Tip: clamp a straight board as a fence — gives perfectly square cuts every time with circular saw.

---

## 3. Bevel cuts

What: cut with the blade tilted, not perpendicular. Used for side top rails (≈12° bevel to match roof slope) and rafter ends.

Why: side top rails need vertical ends so they sit flush against the vertical posts, but their face is sloped. So the end-cut is a bevel.

Watch:
- [Bevel cuts with a circular saw — Mr Adams](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gVq9n-JTowY)
- [Toolstop bevel guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=veinzkhZBCA)
- [45° bevel + miter explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=znQvM59Xt3c)

Tip: set the angle, cut a test piece, check with a protractor or bevel gauge before cutting your real timber.

---

## 4. Pocket-hole joinery ← **chosen method for this build**

What: drill an angled hole into one board, drive a screw through it into the next board. Strong, hidden, beginner-friendly.

Why: joining rails to posts and door-frame stiles to rails. Faster than mortise & tenon, stronger than butt-screws.

Watch:
- [Beginner's guide to pocket holes (Kreg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eLrDC0xYzQU)
- [Pocket-hole tutorial 320 jig](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4cGzVCcDDZA)
- [Pocket Holes 101 step-by-step](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bxRSbg8WAz4)
- [Kreg official "7 simple pocket-hole joints"](https://learn.kregtool.com/learn/video-7-simple-pocket-hole-joints/)

For this build: 70×45 posts and 70×35 rails — use coarse-thread screws (softwood), set jig for 35mm material thickness.

---

## 5. Drilling pilot holes

What: small-diameter hole before driving screw — prevents splitting, keeps screw straight.

Why: timber will split when screwing close to an end. Always pilot near ends, near edges, into hardwood, or with fat screws.

Watch:
- [Pilot holes — when and why](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qPB1aPAItuQ)

Rule of thumb: pilot ≈ 70% of screw shank diameter (so for an 8g screw ≈ 3mm bit).

---

## 6. Router work — grooved door panels ← **chosen for this build**

What: cut a groove down the inside edge of each door stile + rail so a poly panel slides in like a picture frame. Done with a plunge router + straight bit (6mm matches twinwall thickness).

Why: strong, weatherproof door without exposed fasteners on the panel. The look you wanted.

Watch:
- [Router basics — plunge router intro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXJ5rOzGoa4)
- [Cabinet door with grooved frame (router)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u3qzWg8Yj-c)
- [Cutting a groove with a router & edge guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_Lk5n3LXYM)
- [Rebate joints explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zSDgxMxIRR8)

Practical steps for this build:
1. Mark groove on inside edge of each stile + rail — centred, ~10mm deep, 6mm wide.
2. Set router bit projection to ~10mm; clamp an edge guide for offset.
3. Practise on offcuts until your test piece slips a 6mm scrap of poly in with no slop.
4. Stop the grooves on the rails before they exit the ends (stub tenon style) OR run them through and bevel the corner joint — beginner: run-through is simpler.
5. Glue + pocket-screw the frame around the poly panel — **don't glue the poly**, it needs to float for thermal expansion.

Bit & speed tips:
- Use a sharp straight bit. Cheap dull bits burn the timber.
- Multiple shallow passes (3mm each) beats one deep pass — fewer burns, less router strain.

## 7. Cutting polycarbonate

### Twinwall (sides, doors, back)

What: cut to size with fine-tooth blade. Seal the cut channels — top with breathable foil tape, bottom with U-channel.

Watch:
- [Multiwall installation guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H_vZHihwb98)
- [Greenhouse twinwall end-wall install](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lNBYQ5Bc5wg)
- [Twinwall step-by-step short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qxXcXI-MECQ)

### Corrugated Suntuf (roof)

What: cut along corrugations with utility knife in a valley; across corrugations with fine-tooth circular saw blade reversed (or jigsaw).

Watch:
- [Suntuf install — corrugated roofing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SiTjjJtFJ24)
- [Corrugated polycarbonate roof DIY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3JMsLP2Yfc)
- [Suntuf cutting & fixing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I55YdKp0Dps)

Critical:
- Oversize fixing holes by 2–3mm (poly expands ~2.5mm/m with temp swing). Don't overtighten — washer should just dimple, not crush.
- Cap screws (with EPDM/neoprene washers) go through the **ridges** of corrugated, not valleys.

## 8. Hanging doors with tee-hinges

What: surface-mount tee-hinge, long leaf on the door, short leaf on the post.

Watch:
- [How to fit T hinges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KfSJSvPBlgE)
- [Suffolk Latch fitting guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KESjwdp7b_g)
- [Gate tee-hinges install](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9i7qj-7nB1Y)

Rule: top hinge 150mm down from door top, bottom hinge 150mm up from door bottom. Hang the **inactive** leaf first, shim 2–3mm clearance at top/sides, screw, then hang active.

---

## 9. Latches, barrel bolts, window stays

What:
- Inactive door: barrel bolt top + barrel bolt bottom into the rails (drilled hole catches the bolt).
- Active door: surface latch latching into the astragal on the inactive leaf.
- Hatch: window stay or arm holds it open; barrel bolt or hook+eye on each side holds it closed.

Watch:
- [Installing a barrel bolt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mAYNqXkkPgs)
- [Fitting a window casement stay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4VqAVc-r5lM)

---

## 10. Slatted floor

What: 12 × 90×21 H4 decking pieces, gaps for drainage, sitting on 2 × 90×45 H4 bearers.

Skill: just crosscuts to length + screwing down. Pre-drill near ends.

Tip: use 4–6mm gaps between slats (a paint stir stick is roughly 4mm — great spacer).

---

## 11. Finishing (stain / oil)

What: brush or wipe outdoor timber oil onto every face of every piece **before assembly** if you want max life. Otherwise just brush after.

Why: bare softwood outside = 2–3yr life. Oiled = 5–10yr. Polycarbonate is the weather barrier but the timber gets wet from rain hitting it.

Watch:
- [Applying decking oil](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z4xK1XO8tDk)
- [Exterior timber finishing — DIY tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KEcgYJYrZJg)

---

## 12. Anchoring (toddler safety)

What: cabinet ≈110kg, but a 2m-tall structure pulled at the top by a small human can still tip. Anchor to ground or wall.

Options:
- Ground screws / earth anchors at each rear corner (best for free-standing)
- L-bracket + masonry anchor into a wall behind it
- Concrete pavers under bearers + ratchet strap over the top (cheapest)

Watch:
- [Earth anchors for sheds and greenhouses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZK2pe9ZWzZc)

---

## Order to learn / try first

1. Practise crosscuts on offcuts (lots).
2. Practise pilot + screw on offcuts.
3. Practise pocket holes on offcuts (if going that route).
4. Practise one bevel cut.
5. **Then** start cutting real stock.

The first hour of mistakes is cheap. The first cut on real timber should not be your first cut ever.
